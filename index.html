The Premise

What holds a nation together, when nothing forces it to stay?

America's DNA traces the sequence of ideas — consent, rights, and the ongoing argument over who "We the People" includes — that have carried the American premise from Philadelphia into the present day. It is a narrative history in the tradition of McCullough, Philbrick, Meacham, and Tuchman: character-driven, evidence-grounded, and written for readers rather than specialists.

The book unfolds across four parts, following the premise from its founding-era origins through the long, unfinished work of living up to it. Details on structure, chapters, and publication will appear here as the manuscript nears completion.

The Author

Barrett Holt

Barrett Holt is a genealogist, family historian, and retired web developer based in Gulf Shores, Alabama. A Navy veteran and active member of the Sons of the American Revolution and the Alabama Society of Mayflower Descendants, he has spent years tracing the documented lines — family and national — that connect the present to the American founding. America's DNA is his first full-length work of narrative history.
